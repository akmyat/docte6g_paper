\begin{abstract}
    Packet-level network simulators such as ns-3 with 5G-LENA rely on analytical or stochastic propagation models that cannot reproduce site-specific multipath, making them unsuitable for evaluating geometry-sensitive techniques such as massive MIMO beamforming and integrated sensing and communication.
    Ray tracing resolves this limitation but imposes per-query computational overhead that is prohibitive at packet-level simulation rates.
    This paper presents \texttt{sionnart}, a contrib module that embeds the Sionna RT ray tracing engine directly into the ns-3 process via pybind11, exposing deterministic channel information through standard ns-3 propagation interfaces without inter-process communication.
    To make ray tracing practical under mobility, the module combines a displacement-based coherence cache, which reuses channel parameters until a node moves beyond a geometry-derived validity radius, with adaptive virtual receiver generation, which pre-computes channel samples for predicted near-future UE positions in the same batched Sionna RT call.
    In IEEE 802.11ax benchmarks with up to 32 stations, \texttt{sionnart} is $232.3\times$ faster than the socket-coupled \texttt{ns3sionna} baseline in the stationary high-load case and $43.8\times$ faster under high mobility and high load, while keeping GPU memory below 500~MiB.
    Applied to 5G NR scenarios with 20 UEs, mixed application traffic, and $2\times2$, $4\times4$, and $8\times8$ gNB arrays across free-space, urban macro, and urban micro environments, the framework shows that deterministic multipath consistently alters CQI and MCS selection, application throughput, packet loss, and gNB energy trends compared with stochastic propagation models — with the divergence growing in geometry-sensitive urban deployments.
    These results demonstrate that embedded ray tracing can support repeatable, geometry-aware system-level studies of massive MIMO energy and performance trade-offs at full protocol-stack fidelity.
\end{abstract}
