\begin{abstract}
    Future 5G/6G studies increasingly require both packet-level protocol realism and site-specific radio propagation.
    ns-3 and 5G-LENA provide mature discrete-event models for traffic, scheduling, mobility, and the NR protocol stack, but their common propagation backends are analytical or stochastic abstractions that cannot reproduce deterministic blockage, reflection, delay, and MIMO channel structure in a particular environment.
    Sionna~RT provides GPU-accelerated ray tracing for such environment-specific channels, but it does not implement end-to-end network protocols.
    This paper presents \emph{sionnart}, an ns-3 contrib module that embeds Sionna~RT directly into the ns-3 process through pybind11 and exposes ray-traced path loss, delay, and MIMO channel frequency responses through standard ns-3 propagation interfaces.
    To make ray tracing practical during packet-level simulation, \emph{sionnart} combines a displacement-based coherence cache with batched snapshot refreshes, derived MIMO-channel caches, weak-link fallback logic, and adaptive virtual receiver generation that samples likely future UE positions in the same Sionna~RT call.
    We evaluate the framework in two experiments.
    First, on IEEE 802.11ax scenarios with up to 32 stations, \emph{sionnart} is $232.3\times$ faster than socket-coupled \texttt{ns3sionna} in the stationary high-load case and $43.8\times$ faster under high mobility and high load, while keeping GPU memory below 500~MiB.
    Second, in 5G-LENA NR scenarios with 20 UEs, mixed application traffic, and $2\times2$, $4\times4$, and $8\times8$ gNB arrays across free-space, urban macro, and urban micro environments, deterministic ray tracing changes CQI/MCS selection, throughput, packet loss, delay, and gNB energy trends compared with analytical propagation.
    The results show that embedded ray tracing can support repeatable system-level studies of the throughput and energy trade-offs created by massive MIMO deployment in geometry-sensitive radio environments.
\end{abstract}
