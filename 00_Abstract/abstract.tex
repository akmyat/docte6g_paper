\begin{abstract}
    Packet-level simulators such as ns-3 and 5G-LENA provide detailed models of traffic, scheduling, mobility, and protocol-stack behavior, but commonly rely on analytical or stochastic propagation models that do not capture site-specific multipath, blockage, and geometry-dependent channel effects. Conversely, Sionna RT provides GPU-accelerated ray tracing for deterministic channel modelling, but does not implement end-to-end network protocols. This paper presents sionnart, an embedded ns-3 extension that integrates Sionna RT directly into the ns-3 process through pybind11, exposing ray-traced path loss, delay, and MIMO channel responses through standard ns-3 propagation interfaces. To make ray tracing practical during packet-level simulation, the framework introduces a displacement-based coherence cache and an adaptive virtual-receiver mechanism that precomputes likely near-future UE channel samples. The framework is evaluated in two stages. First, IEEE 802.11ax experiments show that the embedded design is up to 232.3× faster than a socket-based ns3sionna approach in stationary high-load scenarios and 43.8× faster under high mobility and high load, while keeping GPU memory below 500 MiB. Second, 5G NR experiments with 20 UEs, mixed application traffic, and multiple gNB array sizes show that deterministic ray-traced propagation changes CQI, MCS, throughput, packet loss, and energy-consumption trends across free-space, urban macro, and urban micro environments. The results demonstrate that embedded ray tracing can support repeatable, energy-aware system-level studies of geometry-sensitive 5G/6G deployments.
