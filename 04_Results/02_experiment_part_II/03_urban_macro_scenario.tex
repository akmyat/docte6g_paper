\subsection{Urban Macro Scenario}
\label{subsec:exp2-urban-macro}

The urban macro scenario is based on the Charles de Gaulle Étoile in Paris, a dense urban environment with tall buildings and complex obstruction patterns.
The gNB is mounted at 25~m (3GPP UMa reference height \cite{tr38901}) near the Arc de Triomphe, and the 3.5~GHz carrier (FR1 n78) is used.
The combination of high building density, sub-6~GHz carrier, and macro-cell distances makes this a representative and challenging 5G~NR deployment scenario.

\subsubsection{Application-related Metrics}
\label{subsubsec:exp2-urban-macro-flow}

Figure~\ref{fig:exp2-urban-macro-flow} shows per-application metrics for the urban macro scenario.
Among data-intensive applications, ns-3 outperforms Sionna~RT at $2\times2$ for FTP ($16.8$~Mbit~s$^{-1}$ vs.\ $15.2$~Mbit~s$^{-1}$), but Sionna~RT surpasses ns-3 at $4\times4$ and $8\times8$ ($19.5$~Mbit~s$^{-1}$ vs.\ $18.5$~Mbit~s$^{-1}$).
Sionna~RT benefits more from larger arrays because ray tracing resolves the multipath structure — reflections and diffractions from surrounding buildings — that enables spatial multiplexing; the stochastic model cannot leverage this geometric structure.
For video streaming, ns-3 consistently achieves higher throughput than Sionna~RT across all antenna configurations.

Downlink packet loss decreases steadily with array size in both simulators.
Sionna~RT shows higher video packet loss ($9.5\%$, $7.6\%$, $4.6\%$ at $2\times2$, $4\times4$, and $8\times8$, respectively); at $2\times2$, ns-3 performs better for video but worse for FTP.
Uplink packet loss is severe in both simulators ($15\%$ for VoIP up to near-total loss for FTP at $2\times2$), reflecting the limited UE transmit power against urban blockage.

\subsubsection{Propagation-related Metrics}
\label{subsubsec:exp2-urban-macro-propagation}

Average path loss is $83.5$~dB and received power is $-37.5$~dBm, nearly identical between the two channel models across all array sizes (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp2-channel-quality}).
The simulators diverge on path delay: ns-3 reports $\approx220$~ns while Sionna~RT reports $\approx360$~ns.
The elevated delay in Sionna~RT is attributable to its multipath-aware propagation model: ray-traced paths include reflected and diffracted components whose longer geometric lengths contribute to the reported delay, whereas the stochastic model reports only the direct-path delay.
This mechanistic difference persists in the urban micro scenario as well (Section~\ref{subsec:exp2-urban-micro}).

Channel quality is consistently lower in Sionna~RT than in ns-3 across all array configurations, and improves with array size in both simulators.
Consistent with the free-space baseline (Section~\ref{subsec:exp2-free-space}), scaling from $2\times2$ to $8\times8$ confirms that larger arrays overcome urban obstruction through beamforming gain.

\subsubsection{Energy Consumption-related Metrics}
\label{subsubsec:exp2-urban-macro-energy}

gNB power scales with both array size and traffic load in both simulators (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp2-gnb-power}).
Under low traffic, both report approximately $25$~W, $40$~W, and $100$~W for $2\times2$, $4\times4$, and $8\times8$.
Sionna~RT reports slightly higher power than ns-3 under high load because its lower predicted channel quality leads to different resource-block allocations and transmission durations.
UE power consumption increases from $0.058$~W at low load to $0.095$~W at high load and is nearly identical between the two simulators, as UEs are constrained to a $2\times2$ array throughout.
